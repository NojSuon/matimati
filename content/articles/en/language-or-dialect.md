You've probably heard it: “in that village they speak a dialect.” At Mati Mati we'd rather say **language**. It's not just about words: it's about respect.

## What is a language?

A language has its own sounds, grammar and vocabulary, and it can express any idea: tell a story, explain a recipe, argue, fall in love. Nahuatl, Yucatec Maya, Mixtec and Otomi do all of that, just like Spanish or English.

## So what is a dialect?

In linguistics, a dialect is a **variety** of a language: the way it's spoken in a region. The Spanish of Yucatán and the Spanish of Sonora are varieties of the same language. Indigenous languages have varieties too, and they're all valid.

The problem starts when “dialect” is used to mean something *less* than a language. That use has no basis, and it has been used to discriminate against the people who speak them.

## What the law says

In Mexico, the **General Law of Linguistic Rights of Indigenous Peoples** (2003) recognizes Indigenous languages and Spanish as **national languages**, with equal standing.

The National Institute of Indigenous Languages (INALI) lists **11 language families, 68 language groups and 364 varieties** in its catalog.
